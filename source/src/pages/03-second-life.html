In this order. Always.

Reuse comes first: a piece worn again can stand in for a new one. Recycling comes next. Energy recovery is for what can't be used any other way.

ReuseWorn again as second-hand{{i:plus}}

Pieces that can be worn again are sold as second-hand clothing.

It's the best outcome: the water and energy that went into making them have already been spent, and the piece keeps using them.

RefashionRepaired or remade by partner workshops{{i:plus}}

A broken zip, a missing button or a small tear can send a piece to a partner workshop.

There it can be repaired, or remade into something new, like a bag or a patchwork jacket.

New fibreRecycled into fibre for new clothes{{i:plus}}

Worn-out pieces of suitable material can be shredded and spun into new yarn.

How much of it ends up in new clothes, we show in the split.

Other productsInsulation, cleaning cloths, filling{{i:plus}}

Fibre that can't become clothing again becomes insulation, cleaning cloths or filling.

It replaces new material in products that last for years.

Energy recoveryFor what can't be used any other way{{i:plus}}

What can't be reused or recycled, like wet, mouldy or heavily mixed material, is used to make energy.

That's why we ask for clean, dry clothes: it keeps this tier as small as possible.

Follow a piece.

Pick something from your wardrobe and see where it could go next. These are possible routes, not a promise for every piece.

Your old jeans

Denim is tough. Jeans that arrive whole usually still fit someone.

  • ReuseIf they still fit someone, they're sold as second-hand.
  • RefashionA broken zip or a torn knee goes to a partner workshop.
  • New fibreWorn thin, the cotton can be spun into new yarn.

Your sneakers

Tied together in pairs, shoes can be worn again. A single shoe can't.

  • ReuseWith life left in the sole, they're checked, cleaned and sold second-hand.
  • Other productsWorn out, their rubber and textile can become new materials.

Your favourite hoodie

It was there for everything. Now it can be there for someone else.

  • ReuseSoft, warm and whole: straight to second-hand.
  • RefashionHoles in the cuffs are patched by a partner workshop.
  • New fibreToo far gone? The cotton blend can be recycled into new fibre.

A party dress

Worn twice, photographed once. It still has Saturdays ahead.

  • ReuseSold second-hand to someone with a party coming up.
  • RefashionA loose seam or a missing sequin gets fixed first.

A worn-out T-shirt

Faded, stretched and a little see-through. Still useful.

  • New fibreClean cotton can be spun into new yarn.
  • Other productsMixed fibres become cleaning cloths or insulation.
  • Energy recoveryIf it can't be reused or recycled.

How we count.

Weighed, not guessed

Every bag is weighed when it arrives. Your figures start from your own kilos, not an average bag.

No number without a source

Water and CO₂e are only shown for the reused share, with the published study they come from right next to the number. Until a source has been checked, we show none.

Rounded and dated

We round, and we say which period a figure covers.

Abroad included

We publish how much is sold as second-hand outside Germany, and where it goes.
